Method and caveats

Banks. Netherlands: ING, ABN AMRO, Rabobank, de Volksbank (now ASN Bank). United Kingdom: Lloyds, NatWest, Barclays, Santander UK. HSBC is left out because most of its profit comes from Asia, so UK rates explain little of it.

Rates. The ECB deposit facility rate is the rate that matters most for euro-area banks with excess reserves. Bank Rate is the Bank of England's policy rate. Dates are effective dates.

Net interest income. As reported in each bank's results. ING includes the TLTRO effect in 2021–22. Lloyds uses its underlying figure because statutory figures were distorted by insurance accounting (IFRS 17). Rabobank 2022 comes from the EBA stress test (regulatory basis), as its own report was not accessible. De Volksbank changed how it presents hedge results in 2025; its 2025 income is roughly €110m higher than it would be on the old basis (based on the size of the 2024 restatement).

Returns. Dutch banks report return on equity, UK banks report return on tangible equity. Compare the trends within each country more than the exact levels across countries. Lloyds 2022 is restated for IFRS 17. Santander UK 2021–22 are adjusted RoTE, as it reported then.

Profit. Each bank's headline figure, so definitions differ slightly (see the Profit basis column). Use it to compare years within one bank.

Causes. The dashboard shows timing and size, not proof of cause. Acquisitions, loan-loss provisions, bank taxes and one-off items (such as ING's 2022 Russia provisions) also move the numbers.
